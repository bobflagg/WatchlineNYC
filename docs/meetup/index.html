(~45s) NYC ownership is fragmented by design. Each building sits in its own single-purpose LLC, usually named after the address — so on paper there's no "landlord," just a scatter of shells. Take Steven Croman: a landlord the AG investigated, who pleaded guilty and served jail time — about as public and documented as a bad actor gets. In Who Owns What's OWN Croman portfolio, those 118 buildings are held under 107 different corporate names — nothing in that list says "Croman." Tools like Who Owns What already re-assemble a lot of this — that's what I build on. But if it takes work to see it for a FAMOUS operator, the quiet ones are effectively invisible — to tenants, reporters, and the agencies meant to hold them accountable.
The thesis

The provenance bar is higher when the output is a person

When the answer is a statistic When the answer is a person — who controls this building
ground truth is published — you look it up ground truth is deliberately hidden (shell LLCs) — you must infer it
a wrong answer is an inaccuracy a wrong answer is a false accusation
it's meant to inform it's meant to be acted on — organizing, journalism, enforcement
"Reasonably incorrect" isn't a tolerable failure mode. Provenance, precision, and "leads, not verdicts" are preconditions — not polish.
(~50s) Hold onto those 107 masks — they're the whole point. There's a real difference between a conversational layer over AGGREGATE data and over OWNERSHIP data. If a number's off by a decimal, you get a bad number — bounded, impersonal, correctable. Our unit isn't a statistic; it's a person — who really controls this building. The ground truth isn't published, it's deliberately obscured behind shell LLCs, so we have to INFER it. And the output is meant to be ACTED ON. Put those together and a wrong answer becomes a false accusation about a named individual. So provenance isn't polish here — it's the precondition for pointing an LLM at this data at all. LINE: "If an ownership tool is wrong, you don't get a bad number — you get a false accusation about a real person."

(~50s) The piece I'd most want you to take away. Underneath is a knowledge graph — but the important part is that every element KNOWS WHAT IT IS: either a directly-sourced fact, or an inference — and if it's an inference, it carries a caveat, automatically, everywhere it's used. Ask "who owns 17 Gay Street?" A naïve tool picks one answer. Ours returns TWO and refuses to blur them. Recorded owner "17 Gay LLC" — a sourced fact, and a shell that tells you nothing. Apparent controller Steven Croman — an INFERENCE, stamped: a lead to verify, not a legal determination. When the two disagree, it says so. This isn't slideware — it's a hard rule in the code: that question always returns both, labeled.

(~60s) Three numbers at three confidence levels — being clear about which is which is the whole ethos. ONE, precision: on a hand-labeled gold set, when it links two records as the same owner it's essentially never wrong — zero cross-surname merges. Croman's 107 LLC names collapse to one. TWO — careful here — is DIVERGENCE, not accuracy. On ~39,000 buildings where both make a call, they disagree both directions: ~780 of my owner-groups pull together buildings WoW splits, ~620 WoW portfolios split across mine. That's how far apart, and which way — NOT a claim about who's right; there's no ground truth in that number. Which is THREE: deciding who's right needs a blind, adjudicated head-to-head. Built — ~530 pairs — but not run. That's the honest gap, and the help I'll ask for at the end. CAVEATS if pushed: divergence≠accuracy; co-op/condo are out of the universe so the splits aren't artifacts; both drivers of the split are real but unjudged.
Credibility anchor #2

Not every "portfolio" is ownership

Shared-address linkage answers "who operates through this office" — not "who owns this"

Linking landlords by shared business address is powerful — often it's the only thread through a shell operation. But it can't tell an owner's office from a managing agent's.

138 different owners register from one office at 770 Lexington Ave (453 buildings).
Read "same office" as "same owner" and you fuse them into one empire that doesn't exist.

Same trap: co-op & condo boards — many unit owners, no single landlord.

The fix isn't to throw away the address signal — it's to stop asking it two questions at once. → three layers

Source: raw HPD registrations, 770 Lexington Ave. Inherent to address-linkage — WoW uses the signal deliberately and flags it on the site.
(~50s) One design decision I want to explain. Linking landlords by shared business address is powerful — often the only thread tying a shell operation together, and Who Owns What uses it deliberately and to good effect. But a shared address can't tell you whether it's the OWNER's office or a MANAGING AGENT's. Raw public record: 138 different owners register from one office at 770 Lexington — 453 buildings. Read "same office" as "same owner" and those 138 collapse into an empire that doesn't exist. Co-op and condo boards do it from the other direction. None of this is a discovery — it's inherent to address linkage, well understood. The question is what you DO about it. Not throw away the signal — it's valuable — but stop making one signal answer two questions.
The architecture

Three layers, not one

A single "portfolio" hides three different questions — so we ask them separately

The question Layer Signal Reliability Live scale
Who manages it? management self-disclosed HPD agent mostly sourced ~28,500 managers · ~67,000 bldgs
What network does it run through? operational nexus shared office / agent / shell (WoW's strength) inferred ~15,800 multi-building nexuses
Who actually owns it? owner identity precision-first record linkage inferred ~6,500 owner-groups
770 Lexington's 138 owners share an operational nexus — real and useful. They are not one owner. Separate layers keep the signal without the false empire.
Owner-identity is inferred (Type II); its accuracy vs. WoW is not yet adjudicated → the blind eval (slide 11).
(~55s) The architectural idea. Collapse everything into one "portfolio" and you're answering three different questions at once — with different answers and different reliability. Who MANAGES is mostly self-disclosed — close to a sourced fact. What operational NETWORK it runs through — WoW's real strength, an inference, deliberately recall-biased. Who OWNS it — a third question, answered precision-first and separately. Back to 770 Lexington: those 138 owners genuinely share an operational nexus — true and useful, we keep it. But they're NOT one owner, and now nothing in the system says they are, because "shares an office" and "same owner" live in different layers. You don't trade recall for precision — you keep both, labeled. Honest note: the owner-identity layer runs — ~6,500 groups — but whether it's MORE ACCURATE than WoW where they disagree is what I haven't adjudicated yet.
Setting up the demo

Conversational — by design, not free-form

The agent orchestrates queries; it doesn't reason about people

Four guarantees, enforced in code — not asked of the model:

  • Asks the graph, doesn't answer from itself — plain English → parameterized, read-only queries. Writes/admin refused, even for the deep tier.
  • Reports only what a query returned — no free-form claims; every inferred element carries its caveat, automatically.
  • "Who owns this?" returns both — recorded owner and apparent controller, labeled — and flags disagreement.
  • Capability is gated structurally — deep investigation only for vetted users; persona sets tone, never access; fail-closed.
The answer to the thesis: you can't manufacture false certainty about a person if you only ever report what a sourced, labeled query returned.
Gating lives in the tool-visibility layer, not a prompt — because the records themselves (raw violation text, ACRIS JSON) are a prompt-injection surface.
(~55s) Before I show you this live, I want to be precise about what it is and isn't. The agent is an ORCHESTRATOR, not a reasoner. It turns your plain-English question into a parameterized, read-only query — it does not free-associate an answer. It can only READ; writes and admin are refused at a guard, even for the deep mode. It reports only what a query returned, with caveats attached. And gating is structural: deep investigation only for vetted users — a self-declared "journalist" doesn't get it; persona only shapes tone. Crucially, that's enforced in the code that decides which tools the model can even SEE — not by instructing the model in a prompt — because the records contain free text, a place an adversary could smuggle instructions. You don't defend that with a polite system prompt. Orchestrator, not reasoner. Now let me show you.
