**Session title:** Who *really* owns this building? Using deeds to uncover the landlords that
ownership data misses

**Format:** Talk + live demo (30–60 min). Works as a panel with a JustFix / Who Owns What
collaborator.

**Skill level:** Intermediate — assumes familiarity with HPD registrations, ACRIS, and JustFix's
Who Owns What.

**Description:**

"Who owns this building?" feels like one question. It's really three — **who owns it, what
operational network it runs through, and who manages it** — and ownership data routinely collapses
them into a single "portfolio." That collapse is the root of the two classic errors in landlord
reporting: **over-merging**, where a shared registration office makes ten unrelated owners look like
one empire, and **under-merging**, where a one-character address typo splits a single landlord across
six portfolios. Keep the three questions separate and both errors disappear.

Separating them also exposes a third problem — the hardest layer, *who actually owns it*, needs a
source registration data doesn't have: the **deed**. This session shows how to read the ACRIS deed
record to uncover shell-LLC ownership that HPD registrations hide, including the shell game's
signature move: buy a whole block out of bankruptcy, then re-deed each building into its own
single-purpose LLC. You'll see real NYC examples, from Steven Croman's 127 buildings unified across
115 differently-named LLCs to a single Queens block that public data reads as six separate owners but
one 2020 deed ties to a single buyer.

Throughout: how to keep these as **investigative leads, not verdicts** by labeling every ownership
link as sourced or inferred, why the hardest job for any of these tools is to *not* wrongly connect
people, and an honest look at the misses — including a building that slips through both the deed
method and Who Owns What.

**What you'll leave knowing:**

- A reusable discipline: separate **who owns** a building from **who operates through this office**
  and **who manages it** — and why collapsing them causes the errors you see in ownership data
- How each of the two classic grouping errors (over-merge, under-merge) maps to one of those
  confusions — so you can diagnose them in your own reporting
- How to read the **ACRIS deed record** to pierce shell-LLC ownership registrations hide, including
  the "buy together, then split into separate LLCs" pattern
- How to label findings as *sourced vs. inferred* so a linkage error becomes a lead to verify, not a
  published mistake
- Where these methods break, honestly — and what public records still can't tell you

**Speaker note:** Independent sabbatical project, built on JustFix's Who Owns What; presented in the
spirit of comparing notes with the housing-data community and finding collaborators.

## Framing notes (internal — not for submission)

- **Why deeds lead the title, three-layer leads the body.** NICAR selects on actionability, and
  reading ACRIS deeds is the one technique a reporter can replicate directly. But the three-layer
  model is the *differentiating* idea — the two classic errors (over/under-merge) are that model in
  disguise (operational-nexus-mistaken-for-ownership; owner-identity-fragmentation). So the body
  opens with the reframe as the organizing insight and delivers deeds as the payoff — novelty *and*
  actionability, not a trade.
- **Examples:** name **Croman** (public / AG-adjudicated); describe the **Haight** block by its
  *pattern* (one block, six owners on paper, one deed) rather than naming the six private individuals
  — a submission is a wide-distribution surface.
- **Honesty beat:** show the misses (the 41-23 recall gap — a building unregistered *and* shell-titled,
  invisible to both the deed method and WoW). NICAR audiences trust speakers who show where it breaks.
- **Panel option:** co-presenting with the JustFix contact strengthens both the submission and the
  "building on WoW, not competing" framing.
- **Do NOT claim more accurate than WoW** — the blind accuracy eval isn't run yet. The honest stance
  is "diverges in both directions, labeled sourced vs inferred," not "beats WoW."
